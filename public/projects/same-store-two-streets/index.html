How to read it

The store needs $1.2 million in sales a year to cover its costs. With an $80 average spend and one visitor in five buying, that takes 75,000 visits a year. On a corner where 12.8 million people walk past, 75,000 is 0.6 percent of the crowd. On a quiet side street with 1.1 million, it's 6.8 percent, more than ten times harder for the exact same store.

That's why location decisions start with the street, not the store: the traffic outside sets how good everything inside has to be.

Where it comes from

At Under Armour I built a scenario model in Excel to size a chain-wide rollout across store tiers: how cost per store and cost per square foot fall as you open more, against break-even benchmarks. This model is an anonymized cousin of that thinking, with illustrative figures. None of Under Armour's numbers are here.

The street side uses public data: two years of hourly counts from Melbourne's open pedestrian sensors, 1.6 million rows cleaned in Python. The 78 most complete sensors are sorted by traffic quality in a Tableau dashboard, so each kind of street feeds the model as a store size instead of a guess.
